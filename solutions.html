Solutions & Action

The crisis is solvable. With the right mix of technology, policy, community action, and individual behaviour change — we can dramatically slash food waste from farm to fork. With 70% of India's food loss originating in rural areas (MoFPI 2022–23), rural-specific solutions are not optional — they are the priority.

Technology-Driven Solutions

From AI demand forecasting to blockchain traceability — technology is reshaping the food supply chain.

🤖 AI / ML

Demand Forecasting with IBM watsonx

IBM watsonx models analyse historical sales, weather, festivals, and regional data to predict demand with 92%+ accuracy. Retailers and distributors can order exactly what's needed — reducing overstock waste by up to 30%.

⛓️ Blockchain

IBM Food Trust

A blockchain-based platform (live with Walmart, Carrefour, Nestlé) that tracks food from farm to shelf in seconds instead of days. When contamination or spoilage is suspected, affected batches can be isolated immediately — preventing mass discards.

📡 IoT

Smart Cold Chain Monitoring

IoT temperature and humidity sensors in reefer trucks and cold stores send real-time alerts when conditions go out of range. IBM Maximo integrates this data to schedule predictive maintenance — preventing breakdown-induced spoilage events.

📱 Mobile

Farmer Price Alert Apps

Apps like Agmarknet, Kisan Suvidha, and custom AI chatbots deliver real-time mandi prices to farmers via SMS/WhatsApp, enabling them to choose the best market and avoid distress selling at exploitative rates.

🛰️ Remote Sensing

Satellite Crop Monitoring

ISRO's FASAL programme and private platforms use satellite imagery to estimate crop yields 4–6 weeks before harvest — giving governments and traders advance warning of gluts, enabling pre-emptive procurement and redistribution.

⚡ Energy

Solar Cold Storage

Off-grid solar-powered cold rooms (e.g., Inficold, CoolBot India) bring affordable refrigeration to villages without reliable power — the single most impactful intervention for reducing primary-source perishable losses.

Policy Counter-Measures

Working with government to create systemic change at scale — the most powerful lever of all.

📋

Mandatory Food Waste Reporting

Legislation requiring large food businesses (supermarkets, hotels, restaurants) to measure and report food waste annually — as done in France (since 2016) and Korea. Public disclosure creates accountability and competitive pressure to improve.

🚫

Ban Supermarket Cosmetic Rejection

France and Portugal have passed laws banning supermarkets from rejecting "ugly" produce on aesthetic grounds. Implementing similar legislation in India would save an estimated 15–20% of fruit and vegetable losses at the retail stage.

🎁

Tax Incentives for Food Donation

India's Income Tax Act allows deduction under Section 80G for food donations to approved NGOs. Expanding this to 150% deduction (as in USA) would incentivise hotels, caterers, and supermarkets to donate surplus rather than discard.

🏭

Mandatory FPO Linkages

Requiring large food companies and government procurement agencies to source a minimum percentage from Farmer Producer Organisations (FPOs) — ensuring farmers get fair prices and reducing the volume that is abandoned due to low returns.

🌡️

Cold Chain as Public Infrastructure

Treating cold storage like roads — government-funded, publicly accessible cold chain hubs at mandis and rural collection centres. Rajasthan's "Rajasthan Warehousing Corporation" model offers a template for other states.

📅

Standardise Date Labelling

The confusion between "Best Before" and "Use By" causes 20% of household food waste. India's FSSAI should mandate clear, standardised labelling with consumer education, reducing unnecessary early disposal of safe food.

♻️

Mandatory Composting for Bulk Generators

Enforce the existing Solid Waste Management Rules 2016 requirement for bulk food waste generators (hotels, institutions, markets) to compost or anaerobically digest organic waste on-site or via contracted agencies.

💻

Digitalise Mandi Arrivals Data

Mandate real-time digital reporting of mandi arrivals and prices (already partially done via eNAM) to enable AI-based early-warning systems for glut prediction and pre-emptive government procurement.

🏘️ Counter-Measures for Rural India

Since 91% of villages lack cold storage (NITI Aayog, 2018) and 68% of marginal farmers sell at harvest-time distress prices (NABARD, 2022), solutions must be grassroots-first, low-cost, and implementable without urban infrastructure. Here are proven, government-supported interventions.

☀️ Infrastructure

Village-Level Solar Cold Hubs

Solar-powered cold rooms (2–5 MT capacity) at the panchayat level eliminate the need for grid electricity. PM-KUSUM Component A provides 60% subsidy on solar installations. Startups like Inficold and ColdBank deploy units at ₹4–8 lakh — affordable via AIF or FPO pooling. Studies by ICAR (2022) show solar cold hubs reduce perishable losses by 40–60% within a 5 km radius.

📄 Supported by: PM-KUSUM (MNRE); AIF (MoA&FW) · Evidence: ICAR 2022
🏪 Market Access

Village Collection Centres & Primary Processing

Primary processing units at the village level — grading tables, crates, weighing infrastructure — add 15–20% to farmer realisation by reducing post-harvest damage before mandi transport. Under RKVY-RAFTAAR (₹7,150 Cr, 2023–24), states can fund Village Level Collection Centres (VLCCs) with sorting, grading, and primary packing facilities accessible to all farmers in a 3 km radius.

📄 Supported by: RKVY-RAFTAAR (MoA&FW, Budget 2023–24) · SMAM (MoA&FW)
🤝 Collective Action

FPO-Based Group Cold Storage & Mandi Selling

Farmer Producer Organisations (FPOs) pool harvests to achieve volume bargaining power, negotiate better mandi prices, and collectively invest in cold storage. The 10,000 FPO Scheme (₹6,865 Cr) provides ₹15 lakh grant + equity + credit guarantee per FPO. As of March 2023, 3,231 FPOs are registered. NABARD data shows FPO-member farmers receive 12–15% higher prices than non-members (NABARD FPO Impact Study, 2022).

📄 Supported by: SFAC / MoA&FW 10,000 FPO Scheme · Evidence: NABARD FPO Impact Study 2022
🌾 Storage

Community Grain Banks & Warehouse Receipt Financing

Community grain banks store surplus grain at village level, preventing distress selling at harvest-time lows. Grameen Bhandaran Yojana (15–25% capital subsidy) and NABARD's warehouse receipt financing allow farmers to borrow against stored grain — removing the compulsion to sell immediately. The Ministry of Tribal Affairs Grain Bank Scheme funds banks in tribal areas; expansion is needed. Currently cover <4% of need (MoTA, 2022).

📄 Supported by: Grameen Bhandaran Yojana (MoRD); MoTA Grain Bank Scheme; NABARD WDRA
👩‍🌾 Women-Led

SHG-Based Food Processing Units (NRLM)

Under DAY-NRLM (9.14 crore women in 83.57 lakh SHGs as of March 2023), rural women SHGs can receive revolving fund and Community Investment Fund (CIF) grants to establish village-level food processing units — pickles, papad, dried fruits, jams, spices. This converts surplus perishables into shelf-stable value-added products with 6–12 month shelf life, eliminating the seasonal waste peak. Units like Lijjat Papad and thousands of NRLM SHG units prove the model.

📄 Supported by: DAY-NRLM (MoRD) · SHG data: MoRD Annual Report 2022–23
📱 Digital Access

Farmer Market Information via SMS & WhatsApp

The Kisan Suvidha app (MoA&FW) and Agmarknet DACFW price portal provide real-time mandi prices to farmers. However, only 38% of rural farmers use smartphones regularly (NSSO 2021). Low-tech solutions matter: IVR price hotlines (like mKisan, with 85+ lakh registered users) deliver prices via basic phone calls in local languages. These tools enable farmers to choose which mandi offers the best price, reducing distress selling.

📄 Tools: Kisan Suvidha (MoA&FW); Agmarknet (DACFW); mKisan (DoAC&FW) · Data: NSSO 2021
🌱 Agronomy

Zero-Budget Natural Farming & Reduced Input Waste

Zero-Budget Natural Farming (ZBNF), supported under the Andhra Pradesh government's RySS programme and adopted by NITI Aayog's Natural Farming Task Force, reduces input costs by 50–60% — meaning farmers are not forced to sell at any price to recover costs. With lower financial pressure, farmers can wait for better prices rather than dumping at harvest. Andhra Pradesh reported 6.5 lakh farmers under ZBNF as of 2023 (AP RySS Annual Report 2022–23).

📄 Supported by: AP RySS; NITI Aayog Natural Farming TF; National Mission on Natural Farming (2023–24)
🛣️ Last Mile

PMGSY Road Connectivity for Market Access

The Pradhan Mantri Gram Sadak Yojana (PMGSY) — with ₹19,000 crore allocation in 2023–24 — is connecting remaining unconnected habitations with all-weather roads. As of March 2023, 7.51 lakh km of rural roads built since inception, but 41% of targeted habitations still remain (PMGSY Progress Report 2023). Each connected village gains the ability to transport perishables to mandi in time — the most basic prerequisite for reducing farm-gate waste.

📄 Supported by: PMGSY (MoRD, Budget 2023–24 ₹19,000 Cr) · Progress: PMGSY Progress Report March 2023
🤖 Technology

Low-Cost IoT Monitoring for Village Cold Rooms

Simple temperature & humidity sensors (₹500–2,000) connected via SMS alerts (no smartphone needed) can notify farmers and SHG managers when cold room conditions fail. IBM Maximo at scale and low-cost equivalents like ESP32 + GSM boards (deployed by ICAR pilot programmes in Odisha and Bihar) have reduced cold room spoilage events by 35% through early warning alerts. ICAR and NHB are supporting replication of this model across 500 cold hubs (NHB Annual Plan 2023–24).

📄 Supported by: IBM Maximo (cold chain IoT); ICAR IoT Pilot (Odisha/Bihar 2022); NHB Annual Plan 2023–24
♻️ Waste-to-Value

Biogas & Composting from Farm Waste

Under the GOBAR-Dhan (Galvanising Organic Bio-Agro Resources) Scheme (MoJSh), village-level biogas plants convert food and agricultural waste into cooking gas and organic manure. Budget 2023–24: ₹10,000 crore for 500 new waste-to-wealth plants in 75 districts. This eliminates the cost of disposing of surplus produce — making it economically viable to harvest even low-value crops, because they can be converted to biogas instead of being abandoned.

📄 Supported by: GOBAR-Dhan Scheme (MoJSh, Budget 2023–24); SBM-G Phase II (MoRD)
📋 Implementation Pathway

How a Village Can Act Right Now — Using Available Government Schemes

  1. Form an FPO → Apply under the 10,000 FPO Scheme (SFAC) → Receive ₹15 lakh handholding grant
  2. Apply for AIF loan (3% interest subvention) → Build a village cold room (2–5 MT solar-powered)
  3. Register on eNAM → Access real-time mandi prices → Sell when price is above distress level
  4. Register women SHGs under NRLM → Access CIF grants → Start pickle/drying/jam unit for surplus
  5. Apply for SMAM subsidy → Purchase crates, grading tables, dryers → Reduce transport damage
  6. Apply for Grameen Bhandaran subsidy → Build storage → Pledge grain for warehouse receipt loan
  7. Apply under PM-KUSUM Component A → Solar power for cold room → Reduce power-cut spoilage
  8. Register on mKisan / Kisan Suvidha → Get daily price alerts → Never sell blind again

All 8 steps are available under existing central government schemes as of 2023–24. Contact your nearest Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK), District Agriculture Office, or NABARD District Development Manager to begin.

What YOU Can Do Right Now

Individual choices aggregate into massive systemic change. Here are 10 high-impact actions.

1

Plan Meals Before Shopping

Buying only what you'll use in the next 3–4 days reduces household waste by an average of 25%.

2

Buy Ugly Produce

Imperfect-looking vegetables taste identical. Choosing them signals market demand, preventing rejection waste.

3

Store Food Correctly

Learn which produce needs refrigeration vs. room temperature. Proper storage doubles average shelf life.

4

Donate Surplus Food

Connect with Robin Hood Army or local shelters to donate cooked/packaged surplus from events and gatherings.

5

Compost at Home

Kitchen composting converts food scraps into nutrient-rich fertiliser, keeping organic waste out of landfills where it produces methane.

6

Understand Date Labels

"Best Before" = quality indicator (food is often still safe after). "Use By" = safety deadline. Don't discard prematurely.

7

Order Smaller Portions

Restaurant plate waste accounts for 8% of all food waste. Ordering à la carte and taking leftovers home makes a real difference.

8

Support FPO Farmers Directly

Buy from farmer markets, organic boxes, or direct-from-farm apps. Removing middlemen improves farmer returns and reduces mandi waste.

9

Advocate & Vote

Support candidates and policies that fund cold chain infrastructure, mandate food waste reporting, and protect farmer MSP rights.
